---
title: "KYC image guidelines"
description: "Image requirements every identity document and selfie must meet to pass the automated KYC review."
---

Documents uploaded for a product enablement request go through an automated compliance review. This page lists what that review expects. Pass these requirements on to the person whose documents you are collecting — they are written as checks you can run against an image before uploading it.

<Warning>
  **There is no fixed turnaround time for KYC review.** Documents are normally checked by an automated compliance flow. If image quality falls short in any way, the case is routed to manual review, which can take **up to several weeks**. Meeting the requirements below is the fastest path to automatic approval.
</Warning>

## Avoid — these send the case to manual review

<Danger>
  **The photo comes straight from a camera and goes straight to the upload. If it passed through a program in between, it will not work.**

  - **Photocopies and scans of the document.** Previously listed as an accepted option — this is no longer the case.
  - **PDF files.** A PDF is what a scanner or a "print to PDF" produces, so it carries the same problem as a scan — and the automated flow expects an image, not a document container. Photograph the document with a camera and upload the image instead. Converting the PDF to JPG in another tool does not help either: the result still comes from a scan, and the conversion itself falls under the editor rule below.
  - **Images opened in Word, Paint, Photos, Preview or any other editor**, even when nothing was changed. Exporting from those tools adds background, margins and metadata that the automated flow reads as editing.
  - **HEIC / HEIF files — the default photo format on iPhone and Mac.** A photo taken with an iPhone is saved as `.heic` unless the camera is set otherwise, and that format is not processed. On the device, go to **Settings › Camera › Formats** and choose **Most Compatible** so the camera shoots JPEG, then take the photo again. Do not convert an existing `.heic` in an image editor — that lands under the editor rule above.
  - **Screenshots.**
  - **Crops, resizes or format conversions made in an editor.**
  - **Frames or white borders** around the image — the "photo of a photo" or "document pasted onto a sheet" look.
  - **Filters, watermarks, annotations or overlaid signatures.**
  - **Digital-only documents issued by government apps.** These are not accepted and will fail the KYC review, including Argentina's **Mi Argentina**, Colombia's **Cédula Digital**, Brazil's **Carteira Digital de Trânsito**, Mexico's **Licencia Digital CDMX**, and Europe's **EU Digital Identity Wallet** / **France Identité** / Spain's **MiDNI**, or any similar national digital-ID app.
</Danger>

## General requirements

<Check>
  - **Format: JPG or PNG.** Other formats are not processed reliably and will fail the KYC review — this includes PDF, and **HEIC / HEIF, which is what an iPhone or a Mac produces by default**.
  - **Size: up to 10 MB per image.**
  - One image per side. Send the back side in `image_back` whenever the document type requires it.
</Check>

## For identity documents

Applies to `passport`, `national_id`, `drivers_license` and `ssn`.

<Check>
  - A photo of the physical document, taken directly with a camera — set to shoot **JPEG**, not HEIC, on Apple devices.
  - The document rests on a clean, flat, contrasting surface.
  - All four corners are visible, and every field is sharp and readable.
  - Lighting is even, with no glare, flash reflections or shadows — watch holograms in particular.
  - The document is valid and has not expired.
  - The back side is included when the document type carries data there (for example, national ID cards).
  - The name and date of birth match the details submitted when the client was created.
</Check>

### The automated check

`passport` and `national_id` images go through an automated image-quality check before they reach the KYC provider, on **both the front and the back**. Like the selfie check, it rejects an image it can't process with a specific, actionable reason (occasionally two, joined by `; `), for example: *"Part of the document is outside the photo. Move the camera back until all four corners are inside the frame, and take the photo again."*

<Check>
  - The image is a readable JPG or PNG, within the size limit above.
  - It has enough resolution to read — a reduced or forwarded copy is rejected.
  - The document is in focus.
  - It isn't washed out by glare or a flash reflection, and isn't too dark.
  - A document is actually visible in the photo.
  - **All four of its corners are inside the frame.**
</Check>

<Note>
  This check looks at the photograph, not at the document. It does **not** read any field, does not verify the document type or the country, does not check the expiry date, and does not detect a photocopy, a screenshot or an edited image — all of those still fail later in the KYC provider's own review. It also does not check orientation: a document photographed sideways passes this check. Passing it doesn't guarantee the document will pass KYC.
</Note>

A document that fails the check is not saved — the requirement stays pending. Fix what the reason names and upload it again.

<Tip>
  The corner check is the one worth designing your capture flow around: it is the single most common reason a document is rejected here, and the fix is always the same — move the camera back until the whole document, including its four corners, sits inside the frame with a margin of background around it.
</Tip>

`drivers_license` and `ssn` are **not** covered by the automated check today; they go straight to the KYC provider, where the same guidance above still applies.

## For selfies

Before a selfie reaches the KYC provider, it goes through an automated image-quality check. The check looks at framing and pose — not identity — and rejects an image it can't process with a specific, actionable reason (occasionally two, joined by `; `), for example: *"Your face is off-center in the frame. Center your face and try again."*

<Check>
  - Exactly one face is visible, sized correctly in the frame and not cut off at the edge.
  - The face is centered.
  - The head faces the camera straight on — no tilting up, down or to the side.
  - The eyes are open.
  - The image is a readable JPG or PNG, within the size limit above.
</Check>

<Note>
  This check validates framing and pose only. It does not check liveness or detect spoofing, does not evaluate sharpness/blur or exposure, and does not check background uniformity. It's a quality pre-filter, not a replacement for the KYC provider's own identity review — passing it doesn't guarantee the selfie will pass KYC.
</Note>

The following still matter for the KYC provider's review, even though they aren't checked automatically:

<Check>
  - The background is plain and free of objects.
  - The selfie is recent and reflects the person's current appearance.
  - No glasses, hats or veils that do not appear in the identity document.
  - The document is not held in frame — it is not required and only adds noise around the face.
</Check>

A selfie that fails the check is not saved — the document requirement stays pending. Fix the issue and upload it again. A **HEIC/HEIF** selfie fails here too, with a clear reason (*"We could not read that image. Upload a JPEG or PNG photo and try again."*) instead of failing KYC review later — this applies to selfies only; other document types follow the general format rule above.

<Tip>
  If a document is rejected, the `rejected_items` array returned by [Complete product documentation](/api-reference/management/individual-clients/complete-product-documentation) carries a `reason` for each item. Fix the issue it names, re-check the requirements above, and upload the image again.
</Tip>
